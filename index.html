Indigenous Learning

Are We Still Listening?

More Than Just Awareness - Rethinking Reconciliation

Timothy Shi · October 5, 2026

In 2013, an Indigenous grassroots movement began, then known as Orange Shirt Day. Fast forward to 2021, when September 30 was formally recognized as the National Day of Truth and Reconciliation. And yet, even after years, some people are still indifferent. Many simply don’t pay attention; words go through one ear and out the other. 

In school, the Land Acknowledgement is played daily over morning announcements. Half the class is hardly awake; for many students, it’s little more than background noise.

To effectively educate about Indigenous peoples, schools must exceed simple presentation of material and create ways to meaningfully engage with the content.

01

Reduce repetition


If someone were to listen to only one song for a week, they would likely soon become bored of it, even if it was their favorite song. Similarly, students can become easily desensitized to something that they hear daily, despite its importance.

Adding some variety to how the Land Acknowledgement is presented could greatly reduce apathy among the student population. As an example, we could encourage learning a new Indigenous word every morning or a new fact about a nearby Indigenous community. The method doesn't matter as much as bringing the attention back.

02

Connect to the real world


Indigenous culture and teachings can be a great way to get valuable insight about our relationship with the natural world. The Thanksgiving Address is a great example that would emphasize gratitude and reciprocity.

Schools could form a connection to everyday things like resource management, overusage, and climate change. Students could see how their learning actually connects to tangible, real things in the world.

03

Incorporate Indigenous voices


Many teachers can often misrepresent or incorrectly teach about Indigenous people. Bringing in Indigenous guest speakers would give insight into personal Indigenous lives and give the general public a better understanding of their culture.
